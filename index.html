Essential Setup

How to Prepare for Accurate Screening

Follow these clinical standard ergonomics for the most dependable preliminary screening results.

Step 1: Distance

Arm's Length Distance

Position yourself approximately 40 – 50 cm (about an arm's length) away from your laptop or monitor. For mobile devices, hold at 35 – 40 cm.

Do not lean forward during the test.
Step 2: Lighting

Optimal Balanced Light

Ensure your room is evenly illuminated with no direct glare, reflections, or harsh sunlight falling onto your screen. Set screen brightness to 70-80%.

Turn off blue light filters/night mode for color accuracy.
Step 3: Covering Eye

Cover Without Pressing

Cup the palm of your hand or an opaque card gently over one eye. Never press against the eyeball or eyelids, as pressure temporarily blurs vision.

Keep both eyes open behind your palm to prevent squinting.
Step 4: Monocular Test

Why Test Each Eye Separately?

The brain naturally compensates using the stronger eye. Testing each eye monocularly uncovers unilateral refractive errors, astigmatism, or amblyopia.

We test the Right Eye (OD) first, then Left Eye (OS).

1. Understanding Screening Results

20/20 vision means you can resolve at 20 feet what a person with standard acuity sees at 20 feet. 20/40 means you must be 20 feet away to read what standard eyes see at 40 feet. Different eyes may naturally vary.

2. Common Vision Difficulties

Myopia (Nearsightedness): Distant objects blur. Hyperopia (Farsightedness): Close objects cause strain. Astigmatism: Imperfect cornea curvature causes ghosting. Presbyopia: Age-related reading difficulty.

3. General Eye-Health Habits

Blink consciously—when staring at digital screens, our blink rate drops from 15 times/minute to only 5–7 times, leading to dry eyes. Keep screens clean and maintain 15–20 degrees downward viewing angle.

4. Screen-Time & Ergonomics

Match ambient room brightness to screen brightness. Avoid using mobile screens in a completely pitch-black bedroom. Position displays approximately 50 to 60 cm away with text size comfortably scaled.

5. Balanced Nutrition for Eyes

Incorporate foods rich in Vitamin A (carrots, sweet potatoes), Lutein & Zeaxanthin (spinach, kale), and Omega-3 fatty acids (walnuts, flaxseed, oily fish) to support retinal photoreceptor and tear-film health.

6. When to Consult a Professional

Seek urgent professional eye care if you experience: sudden loss or dimming of vision, dark curtains in peripheral sight, flashes of light, sudden increase in floaters, persistent ocular pain, or double vision.
